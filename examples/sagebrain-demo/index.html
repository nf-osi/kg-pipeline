Patient JH-2-002: genotype to measured drug response

One patient. One cell line grown from one of their tumours. Connected to drug screens and indications.

setup

Three datasets, one live query

Every number in this demo is fetched live from a read-only query service.

This is a demonstration of what the data can connect for one patient. It already holds the experiment that would have proposed next.
step 1 · genotype

The textbook route to malignancy, in one patient

Four tumours from this patient were sequenced. NF1 p.F2083Pfs*15 is in all four. SUZ12 p.Q424* is in only the two that turned malignant, the second event textbooks associate with this transformation. Nobody matched these by hand. Each is the same change down to the base, identified by a GA4GH VRS id: a checksum of the exact DNA change, not a gene name.

Expect: NF1 p.F2083Pfs*15 in 4 specimens; SUZ12 p.Q424* in 2, MPNST only. Point at the SUZ12 row.
step 2 · model and screening

Their own cells, already screened

A cell line grown from one of this patient’s tumours is filed against the same patient record. Drug screens had already been run on those cells, and each screening file now carries a public ChEMBL drug identifier alongside whatever the lab originally typed, 212= trametinib and all. That identifier is what the next two steps travel on.

Expect: 35 molecules across five of this patient’s specimens. The -CL cohort alone is 26 molecules over 188 files. Point at trametinib, TNO155, ribociclib.
step 3 · mechanism and pathway

No drug reaches NF1. The pathway is the route.

No drug in Open Targets acts on NF1 itself; it is not a druggable protein. What the screened drugs do act on is MAP2K1 and MAP2K2, the MEK proteins, immediately downstream. Reactome is what proves the connection: MEK and NF1 sit in the same signalling chain, the RAF/MAP kinase cascade. Three datasets, one query.

Expect: trametinib, selumetinib, mirdametinib → MAP2K1/MAP2K2; batoprotafib (TNO155) → PTPN11; shared pathways include RAF/MAP kinase cascade and Oncogenic MAPK signaling. Ribociclib appears in the footnote, not the diagram.
step 4 · regulatory status

The drug class is approved, in this exact disease

Joined on the two tumour types recorded for this patient: plexiform neurofibroma and malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour. A clinical stage belongs to a drug and a disease together, so every dot below carries both.

Expect: 16 rows. Mirdametinib and selumetinib approved for plexiform neurofibroma; trametinib phase 2; the MPNST side tops out at phase 3 chemotherapy.
the loop, closed

The graph already held the experiment you’d propose next

  • Genotype. NF1 p.F2083Pfs*15 in all four tumours; SUZ12 p.Q424* in only the two malignant ones. The same DNA change each time, matched by identifier rather than by eye.
  • Measured response. The patient’s own cell line, already screened against trametinib, TNO155, ribociclib and the TNO155 + ribociclib combination, with RNA-seq readout.
  • Mechanism. Trametinib, selumetinib and mirdametinib act on MAP2K1/2, in the same signalling cascade as NF1. Ribociclib’s targets, CDK4 and CDK6, share none of NF1’s pathways. It was screened as half of a combination, and the data says so.
  • Regulatory status. Two MEK inhibitors are approved for plexiform neurofibroma. Trametinib, the MEK inhibitor actually screened on these cells, has reached phase 2 in the same disease.
Caveats, stated on stage. One patient, one derived model. Approval facts belong to a specific indication and population, and are not treatment guidance. Most drug free-text in the portal still does not resolve to an identifier; where a label named several molecules, it was left for a curator rather than guessed.
