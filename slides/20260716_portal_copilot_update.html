Case Study · IKEA

Why a furniture company built a knowledge graph

IKEA wanted to recreate online what its interior designers do in-store: pair products that truly belong together. The obvious approach, learning from what the crowd buys, had a blind spot.

The wisdom of the crowd can't provide the why to the recommendations it gives.

Katariina Kari · Inter IKEA Systems · KGC 2023

Source: Katariina Kari & Adam Keresztes (Inter IKEA Systems), presented at KGC 2023, via metaphacts, “Building Explainable, Trustworthy Recommendation Systems.” ↗
IKEA's Answer

Capture the reasoning only an expert has

What only an expert knows
Rounded corners → safe for homes with babies and young children
On castors → rolls out of the way to free up a tight space
Additional shelf → holds more in less floor area, ideal for small homes
⇒  family-friendly · small-space
Structuring data this way enables IKEA's recommender to make recommendations that are thoughtful, customer-focused and true-to-life.

The graph carries the reasoning, not just the label. Encode it once and every product inherits it, while preventing mistakes like tagging a table with sharp corners “suitable for families.”

Reused again for vision: IKEA feeds the same encoded reasoning into image-based machine learning, linking how a product looks to what its features mean.

Funder Guidance

We need to recommend the way domain experts want

Just like IKEA's interior designers, our domain experts have a specific sense of what makes a tool the right one. This is a real example of what they are looking for when they search for tools.

What this tells us

  • Reusable tool-criteria logic: capture what makes a tool fit once, then reuse that judgment for every recommendation.
  • Search isn't enough: the current OpenSearch retrieval and faceting interface can't answer these questions.
  • Beyond search, into research: answering them takes synthesis and reasoning, not just retrieval.
How It Reasons

Both the ontology and the LLM contribute reasoning to help the user

Deterministic · Symbolic

Symbolic reasoning: imported ontologies and our own

When a requirement can be pinned to explicit criteria, we encode it once, importing an established ontology or building our own, so the category resolves consistently with auditable reasoning.

Imported ontology · BAO
Requirement
metabolic pathway profiling

Which assays actually count as this?

The BioAssay Ontology (BAO) already defines and categorizes assay types, so the assistant can resolve exactly which assays qualify.

Our internal ontology
Requirement
pediatric-relevant model

What does that actually mean, per model type?

Cell lines → check the age of the donor it was derived from
Animal models → check that disease manifestations occur in the developmental stage

Encode the rule once and the category populates automatically, with each entry carrying the reason why it qualifies.

Persona

Why this is not simply search

Each prompt depends on assistant behaviors that go beyond fast ranked retrieval over indexed metadata.

  • Personalization and domain understanding: needs implict interpretation of question and results, comparative judgment.
  • Cross-source synthesis: the answer needs information to be connected across entities and to other sources like literature.
  • Reasoning: the assistant has to "do research" to find the right answer, which requires analytical steps.
  • Guided action: useful output includes explanation and suggested next steps.

A different kind of question

These lean on the portal's help documentation, and the last one checks the assistant knows its scope.

  • Docs grounding: license guidance and submission steps are answered in context, straight from the help docs.
  • Drafting help: the assistant can help compose a strong access request, not just link to the form.
  • Stays on topic: an off-topic ask, like a cookie recipe, should be politely declined and redirected.
Portal Search & Discovery Strategy

OpenSearch and an AI Portal Copilot solve different classes of questions

Two complementary efforts: better search for everyday discovery, and an assistant for the questions search cannot answer.

OpenSearch
Deliverable 1.1.1

Fast discovery over what is already indexed

The first pass: fast keyword and faceted search over existing portal data, at low cost.

  • Fast lookups, filtering, and sorting
  • Autosuggest from current portal vocabulary
  • Semantic flexibility via synonyms and ranking
  • Low cost for everyday search traffic
Best Fit

Known-item search, metadata lookups, and faceted browsing.

AI Portal Assistant
Deliverable 1.1.2

For answers that must be assembled, compared, or acted on

An agent over the knowledge graph and publications, answering what search cannot.

  • Cross-source synthesis, including the knowledge graph
  • Uses publications not indexed in OpenSearch
  • Multi-step reasoning, aggregation, and comparison
  • Suggests next steps and can act on your behalf
Best Fit

Synthesis, comparisons, explanation-heavy and guided workflows.
